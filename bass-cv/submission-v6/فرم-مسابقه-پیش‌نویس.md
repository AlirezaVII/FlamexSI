## Short summary — English draft

Falmex turns real NASA microgravity-combustion data into reproducible scientific findings and evaluates supervised computer vision without hiding negative results. Its FLEX work package standardizes 274 PSI-69 experiments and reports robust observational associations, including oxygen fraction versus the droplet burning-rate constant. Its BASS-II work package trains a LightGBM pixel segmenter for visibly luminous flame regions using video-grouped development and frozen inference on reserved videos. Version 6 improved modestly in development but scored zero true-positive pixels against the initial coarse reference on the new confirmation set. Falmex therefore presents a reproducible research and evaluation framework—not an operational fire-safety system—and makes its limitations, provenance, predictions, annotations, and failure analyses available for review.

## Problem and motivation — English draft

Microgravity combustion datasets contain valuable measurements and videos, but converting them into inspectable scientific evidence requires careful units, provenance, uncertainty handling, leakage-resistant evaluation, and a clear distinction between statistical association and operational claims. Falmex addresses this gap with two connected research routes: droplet-combustion discovery from FLEX tabular data and luminous-region segmentation from BASS-II video. The project treats negative and inconclusive outcomes as reportable evidence rather than removing them.

## Approach and use of AI — English draft

For FLEX, Falmex uses deterministic ingestion, the established \(D^2\) relationship, targeted statistical tests, experiment- and date-separated splits, permutation and bootstrap checks, compositional sensitivity analysis, and literature-based uncertainty propagation. No LLM generates statistical findings.

For BASS-II, Falmex uses a supervised LightGBM pixel classifier with 74 color, spatial, low-light, multiscale, and temporal features. Model selection is grouped by video. Reserved confirmation videos are frozen before inference, and all v3/v4/v5/v6 predictions are hash-frozen before source-only reference annotation and metric inspection. The v6 confirmation failed, demonstrating that development improvement alone is not evidence of generalization.

## Results — English draft

In FLEX, seven rank correlations and one fuel-group difference repeated across the primary and date-split procedures. The oxygen–\(K\) association had Spearman \(\rho=0.577\) in primary discovery and \(0.571\) in holdout; the date-split values were \(0.521\) and \(0.703\). These are observational associations, not causal or externally replicated findings.

In BASS-II v6 development, Positive Macro IoU increased from 0.471908 to 0.483806 relative to the frozen-v5 retrospective reference, using a disclosed fallback because no candidate met all constraints. Against the initial coarse reference on reserved B14/B17/B18 confirmation frames, v6 produced zero TP, Macro IoU, and Micro IoU. V4 and v5 also produced zero TP; v3 retained limited overlap with Macro IoU 0.098404. Scoring the same v6 masks against reviewer A gives 1,873 true-positive pixels and Micro IoU 0.0271. Reviewer B gives 3,272 and 0.0337. The dashboard exposes all 24 frames, references and aggregate results for each model. All three v6 overlap scores are low.

## Impact and next step — English draft

Falmex provides an inspectable path from NASA source data to scientific claims, model outputs, and negative results. Its immediate value is reproducibility, error diagnosis, and prevention of overclaiming. Two user-supplied BASS-II annotation sets have been processed: reviewer A marks seven frames positive and reviewer B marks all 24 positive, with 17 visibility disagreements, positive-union Macro IoU 0.110778, and Micro IoU 0.257337. Dual-reference reporting is complete. The separate A and B masks are retained; no third pixel reference was produced. Reviewer identity, expertise, and independence are not inferred from the files. Any future model cycle must move B14/B17/B18 into development and reserve genuinely new confirmation videos.

## Limitations — English draft

No causal suppression claim, independent-expert endorsement, external operational validation, Lunar/Mars-gravity prediction, or spacecraft readiness is established. FLEX split-holdouts are internal and do not constitute external replication. BASS-II confirmation contains only three video clusters and five positive coarse-reference frames, and v6 has zero TP against that initial reference. The two reviewer sets give different spatial boundaries. Expert approval and a consensus pixel reference remain unestablished.
